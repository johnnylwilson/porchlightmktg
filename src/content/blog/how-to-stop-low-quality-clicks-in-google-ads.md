To cut down on low-quality clicks in Google Ads, tighten your keywords around real intent, review the search terms that already got clicks, and add the bad ones as negative keywords. Then make your ad copy and landing page match the exact services you want, and limit targeting to the area you actually serve. That reduces wasted spend and raises the share of clicks that can turn into real leads.

## Why low-quality clicks happen

Low-quality clicks usually come from one of three problems:

- Your keywords are too broad, so Google matches you to unrelated searches
- Your ads don't qualify the click, so the wrong people still tap
- Your landing page is vague, so it attracts curious clicks instead of buyers

The fix isn't complicated, but it does take consistency.

## Step 1: Use keywords that reflect real intent

Your keyword list should match the services you sell, not generic phrases. A broad term can trigger everything from job seekers to students to people looking for free advice.

Focus on terms that describe the specific service someone wants to hire for, and keep each ad group built around one service.

## Step 2: Review search terms and add negatives

This is the fastest lever you have. Open the Search terms report and filter to terms that received clicks. Any time you see a search that isn't a real buyer, add it as a negative keyword.

Common negative keyword themes for local businesses include:

- free, cheap, DIY
- jobs, salary, hiring, training, course
- how to, templates, examples
- services you don't offer
- towns you don't serve

Over time, this list keeps your spend from drifting toward the wrong people.

## Step 3: Qualify the click in your ad copy

Ad copy isn't only about getting clicks. It's your first filter. Generic ads get more clicks, but often the wrong kind.

- Put the specific service in your headlines
- Mention who the service is for, if that helps people self-select
- Be clear about what's included and typical timing
- Keep the call to action direct, like Request a Quote or Schedule a Visit

Good ads spell out exactly what you offer, which keeps the wrong people from clicking.

## Step 4: Keep location targeting realistic

Showing ads too far from where you work is a common cause of wasted clicks. People may click before realizing you aren't local, or the job may be too far away to be worth it.

Start with the area you actually serve. If you need to cover a larger region, use separate campaigns by area instead of one wide net.

## Step 5: Match the landing page to the search

If someone looks up one particular service and lands on a generic homepage, they're likely to leave. Your landing page should include:

- A clear headline naming the service and who it's for
- What's included and typical timing
- A brief form high on the page
- Easy ways to call or request a quote
- Reviews that mention that service, if you have them
- Licenses, certifications, or experience that build trust

This helps the right people reach out and discourages the wrong ones from taking the next step.

## Step 6: Watch the right numbers

Click-through rate helps confirm your ads match your keywords. Conversion rate shows whether your landing page is doing its job. Both tend to improve early and then level off. After that, the biggest gains usually come from cleaning up search terms and improving the landing page.

## Step 7: Confirm results with a simple monthly sheet

Google Ads can't see what happens after someone contacts you. To know whether things are improving, keep a monthly sheet with:

- Ad spend
- Clicks
- Calls and form submissions
- Jobs that came from ad inquiries
- Revenue from those jobs
