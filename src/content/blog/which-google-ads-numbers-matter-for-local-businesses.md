The Google Ads metrics that matter most are the quality of the searches you're showing up for, the calls and form requests your ads produce, and the return on ad spend you confirm with your own records. Click-through rate is useful for checking whether your ads match your keywords, and time on site helps you judge your landing page.

## 1. Quality search traffic

Clicks only help if they come from the right searches. Open the Search terms report, look at the terms that received clicks, and add any poor ones as negative keywords. Put more of your budget behind exact and phrase matches that describe your services and the areas you serve. A short monthly note on which negatives you added keeps everyone on the same page.

## 2. Conversions

For most local businesses, the conversions that matter are phone calls and form submissions. Track clicks on your phone number and completed forms as your main conversion events. Keep forms short and near the top of the page. Break conversions out by campaign or ad group so you can move budget toward the services that bring real inquiries.

## 3. Return on ad spend you confirm yourself

Google Ads can't see what happens after someone contacts you, so build a simple monthly sheet:

- Month
- Ad spend
- Calls and form submissions
- Jobs closed from ad inquiries
- Revenue from those jobs
- Return on ad spend (revenue from ads divided by ad spend)

Have whoever answers the phone note which new jobs came from an ad. A quick note in your intake log is usually enough.

## 4. Click-through rate as a check

Click-through rate tells you whether your ad copy matches the searches in each ad group. You can usually improve it by reflecting the search in your headline and using sitelinks and callouts. Expect it to level off once your copy and keywords line up. It isn't meant to climb forever.

## 5. Conversion rate

Conversion rate depends on your service, your market, and your landing page. Instead of comparing yourself to industry averages, measure it against your own starting point and watch whether it improves as you make changes.

## 6. Time on site

Time on site, tracked in your analytics, is a useful sign of landing page quality. To improve it:

- Make the headline and first paragraph match the search and say who the service is for
- Add clear buttons near the top and near any proof or pricing details
- Place a short form near the top of the page
- Add reviews that mention the service and the area
- Show licenses, certifications, or experience
- Explain what's included and the typical timeline

When engagement goes up, conversion rate often follows without changing bids.

## 7. A simple monthly routine

1. Pull spend, clicks, and conversions from Google Ads
2. Review search terms and add negatives for poor searches that got clicks
3. Match up closed jobs and revenue that came from ad inquiries
4. Update your monthly sheet and shift budget toward the best-performing services

## Bottom line

Judge your [Google Ads](/google-ads) metrics by who clicked, who contacted you, and what work actually came in. Keep an eye on search quality, count calls and form requests, and confirm your return each month with your own records. Those habits keep your budget pointed at real customers.
