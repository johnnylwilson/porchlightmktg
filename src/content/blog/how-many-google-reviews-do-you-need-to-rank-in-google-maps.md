There's no single number of Google reviews that gets a business into the Google Maps results. A practical target is the median review count of the top three businesses in your market, plus a 20 to 30% buffer. In a small town that might be 15 to 30 reviews. In a mid-sized city it's often 40 to 80, and in a large metro it can be 100 or more. Rating, recency, and how you respond matter too.

Google ranks businesses in Maps using relevance, distance, and prominence. Google reviews mostly feed into prominence, one of the factors behind Maps rankings, and they have a big effect on whether people click. So the right target isn't a fixed number. It's whatever it takes to keep pace with the businesses already showing up for the searches you care about.

## Set your target with a quick check

1. Pick five to eight searches that bring you your best work, such as your main service plus your city.
2. For each search, write down the review count and rating of the top three businesses in the Maps results.
3. Find the median of those counts across all your searches.
4. Set your target at that median plus 20 to 30%, so you stay competitive as others keep adding reviews.

For example, if the median of the top three is 52 reviews, aim for 65 to 70. In a smaller town where the median is 18, aim for 22 to 24.

## Quality and steadiness beat raw totals

Review count is only part of the picture. These signals often decide close calls:

- **Average rating.** A rating in the mid-4s or higher helps people feel comfortable calling. A low rating can hurt even if you rank.
- **Recency.** A steady stream of reviews looks better than a burst followed by months of silence. In many markets, a few new reviews a month is a healthy pace.
- **Detail in reviews.** Don't script anything, but it helps when customers mention what you did for them and where.
- **Your responses.** Reply to every review within a few days. Keep it natural and sign with a name.
- **A complete profile.** The right category, services, hours, photos, and service area all help your reviews do their job.

## How to earn reviews without nagging

Ask at a natural moment, right after the job is done or the customer says thanks. Make it easy:

- Send a short email or text with your direct review link.
- Offer one line of guidance, like "If you have a minute, mention the service and your town so others know what we did."
- Never offer incentives and never filter out unhappy customers. Review gating goes against Google's policies.

If someone had a bad experience, give them an easy way to reach you privately. Fixing a problem quickly protects your rating and often leads to a better review later.

## Keep an eye on what matters

- Your total review count compared with your target
- How many new reviews you get each month
- Your mix of 5-star and 4-star reviews
- Calls and requests that come from your Maps listing

## Bottom line

There's no universal number. Look at who already shows up in your market, aim a little above the middle of the pack, and keep a steady flow of genuine Google reviews coming in. Pair that with a complete Google Business Profile, quick responses, and a website built on solid [local SEO](/local-seo), and you give your business a much better chance of showing up when nearby customers search.
