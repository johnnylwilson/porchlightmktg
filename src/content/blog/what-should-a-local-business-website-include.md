A good local business website should make three things clear right away: what you do, where you work, and how someone can contact you. From there, dedicated service pages, reviews, location details, simple contact forms, and proof of your experience help turn more of those visitors into real leads.

## Make it obvious what you do

Someone shouldn't have to scroll halfway down your homepage to figure out what your business does.

The top of the page should quickly explain your main service, where you offer it, and what the visitor should do next.

You don't need a clever headline or a paragraph about how passionate you are about customer service. Clear usually wins. If you serve Orlando and the surrounding towns, say that. If you focus on a certain kind of work, make that clear too.

## Give your main services their own pages

One mistake we see often is putting every service on a single page.

Say you run a cleaning company that offers move-out cleaning, deep cleaning, recurring service, and office cleaning. Each of those should have its own page instead of being squeezed into a few paragraphs on a general services page.

This gives potential customers more information about the exact service they need. It also gives each service a better chance of showing up in Google when someone searches for it.

You can still have a main services page that lists everything you offer. Use it to send visitors to the more detailed pages.

## Make it easy to contact you

If someone decides they want to talk to you, don't make them work for it.

Your phone number should be easy to find and tappable on mobile. You should also have clear buttons that take people to your contact form or scheduling page.

We like offering two options, such as a free consultation and a contact button, because not everyone wants to take the same next step.

You don't need a button after every other paragraph, though. A few well-placed calls to action are enough.

Keep your contact form simple:

- Name
- Phone
- Email
- Message

You can always get the rest of the details after someone reaches out.

## Show people why they should trust you

A website can look great and still feel completely anonymous.

Give people a reason to feel comfortable contacting you. Depending on your business, that could include customer reviews, years of experience, licenses, certifications, photos of your team, or examples of your work.

Testimonials are especially useful because they come from real customers instead of from you.

Don't hide all of this on an About page. Put trust signals throughout the site where people will actually see them.

## Make your location clear

For a local business, your website should make it obvious where you're located and which areas you serve.

If you have a physical office, include the address in places like your footer and contact page. Embedding your Google Business Profile map can also make it easy for visitors to find you.

Businesses with more than one office can create a page for each real location. If you serve surrounding cities without offices there, location pages can make sense too. Just explain the services you provide in that area without pretending you have an office there.

## Include reviews throughout the site

You don't need to dump 50 reviews onto one page and call it a day.

Put a few strong reviews on your homepage. Add relevant ones to service pages. You can also include reviews near contact forms, where someone may be deciding whether to reach out.

The idea is to show proof at the moment someone is making a decision.

## Don't forget about mobile

A lot of people looking for a local service are doing it on their phone. They might be sitting in their car or quickly comparing a few businesses before making a call.

Your website needs to work just as well on a phone as on a desktop. Text should be easy to read, buttons easy to tap, forms quick to fill out, and your phone number tappable.

Speed matters too. If a page takes too long to load, there's a good chance people will go back to Google and try someone else.

## Your website has a pretty simple job

A local business website doesn't need to be complicated. It needs to help the right person understand what you offer, decide whether they trust you, and easily take the next step.

That's what good [website design for local businesses](/website-design) should focus on. Every page, review, form, and button should help turn visitors into potential customers.
