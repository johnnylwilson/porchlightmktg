Most local business websites lose leads because they make visitors work too hard to understand what you offer, where you work, and how to get in touch. The biggest problems are vague service pages, weak location details, missing proof, and a contact path that's buried or too long. Fixing these can bring in more calls and form requests from the traffic you already have.

## Mistake 1: Your services are too vague

If your site only lists a general category, people can't tell whether you handle the specific job they need. They leave or hesitate.

**Fix:** Give each main service its own page. Say clearly what the service is, who it's for, and what's included. Use plain language.

## Mistake 2: You don't explain what people get

Customers want to know what they're paying for before they call.

**Fix:** List what's included on each service page, and add a short note about typical timing and what affects it.

## Mistake 3: There's no easy way to reach you

This is the most common problem. If your phone number is hard to find or your form is long, people drop off.

**Fix:**

- Put a short form near the top of service pages and your contact page
- Keep it to four to six fields
- Place clear buttons near the top and again further down the page
- Make your phone number tappable on mobile

## Mistake 4: Proof is missing where it matters

Many sites have a nice homepage but no proof on the pages people actually land on.

**Fix:** Add reviews that mention the specific service to each service page, and show licenses, certifications, or years of experience in a short block.

## Mistake 5: Your location is unclear

Local customers want to know you're real and nearby.

**Fix:** Put your address and an embedded map on your homepage and contact page, keep your business name, address, and phone number consistent, and add a short "Serving Lake Mary and nearby towns" line on key pages.

## Mistake 6: Your city pages are copies

Location pages can bring in leads, but copy-and-paste pages rarely rank and can make the site feel spammy.

**Fix:** Make each city page unique. Include the services you offer there and anything useful for someone in that area.

## Mistake 7: There's nothing to support your service pages

Blog posts that answer real questions help your service pages show up and help visitors decide.

**Fix:** Write posts that answer common questions for each service, give a short, direct answer near the top, and link back to the related service page.

## Mistake 8: Your About page doesn't build confidence

People often check your About page before reaching out.

**Fix:** Include who you are, how long you've been in business, licenses and insurance, the areas you serve, and real photos and short bios of your team.

## Mistake 9: The site is slow on phones

Many visitors are on their phones. If the page takes too long to load, they leave before they call.

**Fix:** Compress images, delay loading of maps and heavy elements, and skip big sliders and extra scripts.

## Mistake 10: The menu hides the important pages

If visitors can't find your services or contact page quickly, they leave.

**Fix:** Keep the menu simple, give each service its own link, and keep a contact button visible in the header.

## Bottom line

Most website problems aren't design problems. They're clarity problems, and fixing them is what good [web design](/website-design) is for. When your site clearly explains your services, shows what people get, proves you're trustworthy, and makes it easy to reach out, you capture more leads from the visitors you already have.
