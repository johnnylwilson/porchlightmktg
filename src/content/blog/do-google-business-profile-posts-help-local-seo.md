Google Business Profile posts don't appear to be a direct ranking factor, so posting on its own probably won't move you up the map pack. Where they help is with the people who already found your listing. A recent, useful post shows your business is active, answers questions before someone calls, and gives them a reason to choose you. That makes posts a small but worthwhile part of a local SEO plan.

## What Google Business Profile posts are

Posts are short updates you publish from your Google Business Profile. They show up on your listing in Google Search and Google Maps, usually with a photo and a button like "Learn more" or "Call now."

There are a few main types:

- **Updates** for news, tips, recent projects, or new blog posts
- **Offers** for a discount or limited-time deal
- **Events** for something happening on a specific date

Older posts eventually drop out of view, so a profile that hasn't posted in months can look a little quiet next to one that posts regularly.

## Do posts actually improve rankings?

The honest answer is: not much, if at all. Local SEO practitioners have tested this, and the common finding is that adding posts didn't produce a measurable lift in map pack rankings. Google's own guidance on local rankings focuses on relevance, distance, and prominence, and doesn't call out posts as a factor.

That doesn't make them useless. It just means they're not the lever to pull first if you're not showing up at all. Your categories, services, reviews, and website pages will do more for where you rank.

## Where posts do help

Posts earn their place after someone finds you. Think about how people compare local businesses. They look at a few listings, glance at reviews and photos, and pick the one that feels most trustworthy and active.

A good post can tip that decision:

- It shows your business is open and paying attention
- It answers a common question before someone has to call
- It shows real work, which builds trust faster than a generic description
- It gives people a clear next step, like reading a guide or requesting a quote

More clicks and calls from the same number of profile views is a real win, even if your position on the map never changes.

## What to post

You don't need to be clever. Useful and specific beats polished. Good options for most local businesses include:

- A recent job, with a real photo and the town it was in
- A quick answer to a question customers ask all the time
- A seasonal reminder, like getting the AC serviced before summer
- A new blog post, with a short summary and a link
- A new service or expanded service area
- An offer, if you actually have one

Use your own photos when you can. Pictures of your team and your work tend to feel more trustworthy than stock images.

## How often to post

Once a week is a solid target. If that's not realistic, every two weeks is fine. Consistency matters more than volume, because the goal is for your listing to always look current to the next person who finds it.

A simple way to stay on track is to tie posts to things you're already doing. Finished a job you're proud of? Post it. Published a blog article? Share it. Running a seasonal special? Post it.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Stuffing posts with keywords or city names. It reads poorly and doesn't help rankings.
- Posting the same generic message over and over.
- Using phone numbers in the post text, which Google may reject. Use the call button instead.
- Letting months go by between posts.
- Expecting posts to fix bigger profile or website problems.
